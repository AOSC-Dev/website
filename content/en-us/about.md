The Anthon Open Source Community (AOSC) was founded in late 2011. It is an open-source community primarily active online, organized through the voluntary efforts of computing enthusiasts. Our main objectives are divided into three areas: technical, cultural, and social.

On the technical side, the community's main project is AOSC OS, a Linux distribution designed and maintained with the goal of being "simple and reliable". AOSC OS is primarily aimed at users with some prior Linux experience, with the desktop experience optimized for personal devices. We are committed to providing users with an out-of-the-box, easy-to-manage, and reliable working environment. While maintaining AOSC OS, we are also developping side software projects such as oma, a package management frontend, and libLoL, a LoongArch old-world to new-world compatibility layer, to further enhance the system experience. As a community whose contributors are primarily native Chinese speakers, we also work to improve the experience for Simplified Chinese users through font config and rendering optimization, direct participation in upstream software localization, and software selection, among other efforts.

On the cultural side, our name "Anthon" (安同) means "settling into collaboration among classmates" (安于同学合作). "安" (An, means "settled"): our community is committed to establishing and maintaining a people-centered core culture. While advancing our daily work, we never forget to maintain good interpersonal relationships among our contributors and members - in our community, we are friends first, colleagues second. "同" (Tong, means "classmates"): technical accumulation comes from practice, and technical development cannot happen without sharing and mentorship - regardless of age, learning from each other is the path to progress. Only by settling into collaboration among classmates can we avoid isolation and stagnation.

On the social side, our community places great importance on collaborative progress and skill-sharing within the open-source software ecosystem. As a community whoses members have diverse backgrounds, we have participated in social outreach in various ways since our founding in 2011, working together with Linux enthusiasts, campus clubs, and enterprises across different regions to promote the widespread adoption of open-source software. Since 2015, the community has held an annual summer gathering called AOSCC, inviting community contributors, members, campus clubs, enterprises, and individuals to participate. While promoting the community's work to the outside world, AOSCC also provides a platform for enthusiasts and industry representatives to share technical skills, industry knowledge, and trends. In recent years, our community has also served as mentors, arranging for contributors to participate in domestic and international open-source project initiatives each year, such as the "Summer of Open Source" program organized by the Institute of Software, Chinese Academy of Sciences, guiding students from universities at home and abroad to develop their application and development skills and gain professional experience.

## Major Projects

Our community's major projects are designed and developed around two operating system projects, AOSC OS and Afterglow, alongside a range of supporting applications, utilities and tools for development and maintenance.

### AOSC OS

AOSC OS is a Linux distribution designed and maintained with the goal of being "simple and reliable". It is primarily aimed at users with some prior Linux experience, with the desktop experience optimized for personal devices. We are committed to providing users with an out-of-the-box, simple and reliable working environment.

AOSC OS supports a wide range of processor architectures, including x86_64, AArch64, MIPS-based Loongson 3, LoongArch (New World), as well as emerging architectures such as RISC-V. At the same time, we also conduct experience testing and optimization for different device types, devote to deliver an out-of-the-box experience across various architectures and device categories.

### Afterglow

Afterglow is developed based on AOSC OS. Through feature trimming, configuration tuning and curated software selection, Afterglow is dedicated to providing ongoing software and technical updates for a variety of older devices — maintaining a good experience and performance to give aging hardware a new lease on life, letting users "rekindle their old favorites."

### oma

oma is a package management frontend focused on simplifying operations, reliability, and efficiency. It is compatible with APT in terms of operation and functionality, and builds upon this foundation to introduce features such as history browsing, operation rollback, and file search.

### libLoL

libLoL (LoongArch on LoongArch) provides compatibility for the old-world ABI, which is commonly used by commercial software designed for Loongson's official Loongnix reference distribution and UOS by UnionTech, such as Tencent QQ, Kingsoft WPS for Linux, and the Loongson Browser. Since these applications have not yet been ported to the new-world ABI, this runtime aims to provide new-world distribution users with the convenience of running such applications.

### Ciel

Ciel is a containerized package build environment management tool used to standardize package build environments, ensuring feature parity and content reliability.

### Autobuild4

Autobuild4 is a semi-automated package build tool that automatically downloads, builds and generates packages based on definition files and scripts. It also supports the use of build and command templates to simplify the process of writing build scripts.

## History

The Anthon Open Source Community (AOSC) originated as the Anthon Development Team (Anthon.Dev), founded by Bai Mingcong and two of his classmates in late 2011. Due to poor management, the development team dissolved within a year, and was reorganized as an open-source community in late 2012, continuing to today.

Our main project, AOSC OS, was originally named "AnthonOS" and was an openSUSE-based distribution built with the now-deprecated SUSE Studio. A year later, development shifted to a Debian-based derivative, with a focus on CJK (Chinese, Japanese, and Korean) support and a custom user interface designed around KDE 4. In 2014, with the help of Icenowy Zheng, the former developer of Heartl Linux, the community launched the project with the name of Anthon\:Next, transited AnthonOS to an independently built distribution. After becoming independent, AnthonOS was renamed to AOSC OS, the general-purpose Linux distribution our community maintains today. Over the following years, maintainers continuously expanded the size of AOSC OS's software repository and introduced multi-architecture support. Today, thanks to the ongoing contributions of our developers, AOSC OS's software and hardware support continues to grow, and numerous supporting projects have been launched around it.

Our annual gathering, AOSCC, began as a summer contributor meetup in 2014, and evolved into an organized in-person summer gathering starting in 2015, hosted at venues sponsored by various enterprises and academic clubs. Each year, the gathering invites relevant figures from the open-source software industry to participate and share their experience, and is open free of charge to community friends from all regions.
